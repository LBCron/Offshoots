Art & color

A fictional educational series from the late 1960s and early 1970s. The covers should look like they came from the same publisher, with a different designer for each lesson.

Cover composition

Use one visual idea that expresses the class’s listening revelation. Work in a square, with large flat forms, strong negative space, restrained typography and a limited period palette. Look at neighboring covers before repeating a device. Let composition distinguish the covers, even when they share a ground color.

Type and print

Keep the title and subtitle in a consistent upper-left footprint. Use a heavy condensed uppercase title and a bold, readable subtitle. Leave clear space above the artwork and keep lettering legible at thumbnail size. Use subtle print grain and slight ink wear. If a brief calls for a worn sleeve, keep the frayed edge thin and the artwork full bleed.

Cover color rules

Use the assigned shelf ground and ink when the cover brief specifies them. Keep the ground flat and the text in its assigned ink. Choose a few contrasting accent inks for the concept. Use transparent overlaps selectively to suggest layered screen printing. The finished cover should read at small sizes before texture is added.

Atlas family colors

The map hue comes from the shelf’s cover ground, lifted to read on the dark map. For near-black covers, the accent supplies the hue. The panel uses a dark ground or a pale wash of the same family color. These are the exact colors in the current Atlas source snapshot.
